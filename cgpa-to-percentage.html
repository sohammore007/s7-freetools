How to Convert CGPA to Percentage

Understanding how to convert your Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) into a percentage is essential for applying to universities, higher education programs, and various job applications. Because grading systems differ globally, and even regionally, the formula to translate a 10-point scale CGPA into a 100-point percentage scale varies.

The most widely accepted conversion formula, popularized by the CBSE educational board and adopted by many Indian universities, is to multiply your CGPA by 9.5. For instance, if you scored an 8.5 CGPA, your percentage would be 8.5 × 9.5 = 80.75%.

However, other institutions may use a direct × 10 multiplier, where an 8.5 CGPA simply equals 85%. Some universities even employ complex non-linear formulas. Because of this discrepancy, our free CGPA Calculator includes a standard 9.5 multiplier, a 10 multiplier, and a Custom Multiplier option so you can apply your institution's specific rule.

Disclaimer: Formulas vary by institution — always check with your specific university or examination board for the official conversion method if this is for an official application or transcript.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard CGPA to percentage formula?

The most common formula, historically used by CBSE and many Indian universities, is to multiply your CGPA by 9.5. For example, a 9.0 CGPA would be 9.0 × 9.5 = 85.5%.

Does every university use the ×9.5 multiplier?

No. While 9.5 is standard for many institutions, some universities use a direct ×10 multiplier, and others have completely custom non-linear formulas. Always check your university's official guidelines.

Is this calculation official?

This calculator provides an accurate mathematical conversion based on the multiplier you select. However, it is a utility tool, and you should use your official university transcript or formula for formal applications.

How do I convert Percentage back to CGPA?

To convert your percentage back to CGPA, simply divide your percentage by the multiplier (e.g., divide by 9.5). Our tool has a reverse toggle that does this automatically for you.
